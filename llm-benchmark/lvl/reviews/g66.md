# Review g66: Alurentell (A, on the draw) vs Boros Aggro (B, on the play)

## 1. Result and turning point
B won on game turn 11 (A at 1, lethal attack). A never had 3 mana on the battlefield. It died holding Show and Tell, Omniscience, Acererak, Aluren and 2 Force of Will.
- The mana denial was B's plan and it worked. B's Wastelands hit Hedge Maze (B 28-29, turn 5) and Tundra (B 36-37, turn 7). The deck has one basic, and Island was A's only land that lasted. Each turn A had Island plus the land it had just played (2 mana), and Show and Tell needs 3. A drew no Lotus Petal in about 14 cards. That is mostly variance.
- Turn 3 (A 19): Voice of Victory went on the stack and A passed with Force of Will open. A could have pitched the spare Show and Tell, and it knew from the turn-2 Ponder that a second Force was two draws down. Voice did 9 of the 18 combat damage A took (1 + 2 mobilize tokens on turns 5, 7 and 9). Without it B would have cast Ajani on turn 3, so the real saving is smaller, but A would still have had life to spare by turn 10. It also took away every instant window on B's turns.
- Turn 8 (A 41-42): A held Savannah to "deny Wasteland" and skipped the land drop (self-flagged). B had no Wasteland on turn 9 (it played Karakas), so Savannah would have survived. A then drew Misty on turn 10, which gave Island + Savannah + a Misty fetch = Show and Tell for Omniscience, then free Acererak with 2 free Forces against 2 Swords. That line needed life to crack Misty, and A was at 1 because of Voice. With both fixes (Force on Voice, land on turn 8) A probably wins on turn 10.

## 2. First real mistake per side
- A: the keep (A 2, self-flagged) was close but defensible. It had 2 lands, Ponder, Force and two engines, and the payoff came by turn 4 (Ponder). A 6-card hand faces the same four Wastelands. The playbook's no-payoff mulligan covers one land plus Ponder, or Show and Tell plus Acererak with no cantrip, and this hand is neither. The fetch (A 9) was right: Island was the only target Wasteland could not hit, and Tropical Island would have died at once. Hedge Maze on turn 4 (A 25) was also right. All three lands in hand were nonbasic, the Island was already tapped for Ponder, and Maze was the least valuable land to feed to Wasteland ("play it on a turn where the untapped land's mana would go unused"). Ponder on turn 4 (A 22-24): no land was in the look, and A already had 3 lands in hand. Ponder draws its top card at once, so either order gave A both Omniscience and Acererak by turn 6, and the order did not matter. Keeping the look over shuffling was right. A's first real mistake was not using Force on Voice (A 19, close). The clear one was the skipped land on turn 8 (A 41). Holding a land never puts more lands on the battlefield at once. It only gives up the turns when the opponent has no Wasteland. It also cost A a discard: it threw away Show and Tell instead of a land.
- B: none. Turn 1 Wasteland as its land (it had no 1-drop), Voice first among its 2-drops, and each Wasteland used on its own turn on A's newest nonbasic. On turn 7 it cast Sand Scout before playing and sacrificing Wasteland, so the Wasteland going to its graveyard made a Sand Warrior. B's own flag (B 12) is moot. Wasteland cannot target a basic. The option existed only because B's own lands are legal targets (the target menus at B 29 and B 37 list Plateau, Savannah and Wasteland). Swords: A never had a creature, and holding both Swords for Acererak or Atraxa (with Karakas) was right.

## 3. Candidate Alurentell playbook rules
1. "Against Wasteland, do not hold a land in hand to protect it: you can still play only one per turn, so holding it gains nothing, and every turn they lack a Wasteland is a land you keep. Exceptions: City of Traitors, or a basic or fetch to play instead." Evidence: A 41-42, turn 8 (B had no Wasteland on turn 9). Confidence: high. It refines "Play a land every turn you have one" (the batch 2 rule), which A broke on purpose for a Wasteland reason the rule does not address.
2. "Against Boros, Force of Will on Voice of Victory (pitch a spare Show and Tell or cantrip) is worth it when your combo is two or more turns away. Voice shuts off every instant on their turns and adds about 3 damage a turn with mobilize." Evidence: A 19, turn 3. Confidence: low-medium, for Brady to rule on. It does not contradict "Spend Force of Will on what breaks the loop", which is about the combo turn. It extends the Boros note "Voice of Victory stops your spells on their turn".

## 4. Candidate Boros notes
1. "Cast Sand Scout before you play and sacrifice Wasteland (or crack a fetch) that turn: the land going to your graveyard makes a Sand Warrior." Evidence: B 34-37, turn 7. Confidence: medium-high. This is new.
2. "Alurentell has one basic land. When they are stuck on 2 lands, Wasteland each new nonbasic on your own turn and keep them below Show and Tell's 3 mana. Wasteland cannot hit their Island, so do not hold it for one." Evidence: B 28-29 and B 36-37, plus B's moot flag 12. Confidence: medium. This refines "Hit Ancient Tomb or their only blue or green dual".

## 5. Process and engine flags
- A skipped its land drop on turn 8 (A 41 main 1, A 42 main 2). No upkeep cantrips, and no wasted tool calls of note. A made 2 sim calls, both on the keep.
- The engine offers "Activate Wasteland" even when the only legal targets are your own lands (B 8, B 12). That is not a bug, but it is a trap: B believed it could hit the basic Island.
- A's report calls B's tapped creatures on A's turn a "stale display". The display was correct: attackers and tapped lands stay tapped until B's untap step.
- B's report says "sim calls 2 though I made none". The counter in the log covers the whole game and includes A's 2 sim calls.
- B's attacks were declared one creature at a time, which took up to 7 prompts per combat. It caused no errors.
