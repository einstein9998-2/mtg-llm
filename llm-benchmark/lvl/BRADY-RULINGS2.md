## Keep or mulligan: think through the first turns (Brady, game 2 of the interactive match)
When deciding whether to keep, write down the plan for the first two or three turns with the hand: what you play and cast on each turn, in what order (cantrip before land, fetch shuffles), and what you are hoping to find. A hand is a keep only if that plan works.

## Ponder order with Show and Tell + Carpet of Flowers (Brady, game 2 of the interactive match)
Ponder showed Show and Tell, Atraxa and Carpet of Flowers; I put Show and Tell, Atraxa, Carpet on top. Brady: that order was punished by Wasteland (my Tropical died the next turn), because Carpet on top would have been a castable play on turn 3 with Tomb; the order should weigh what the mana allows next turn, not only the two-card combo (Show and Tell + Atraxa was not castable safely before the Chant turn anyway).

## Fetch after the draw when a known card sits on top (Brady, game 2 of the interactive match)
With Carpet of Flowers known on top from Ponder, I cracked Misty at the opponent's end step and shuffled it away. Brady: wait to fetch until after the draw, so the known top card is drawn first (fetching in the main phase after the draw also still keeps the fetch hidden from a Wasteland). Cracking at the end step is right only when the top card is unknown or unwanted.

## Tool note: floating mana is spent first, including on generic costs (found in game 2 of the interactive match)
The engine's automatic payment spends pool mana before lands and uses it for generic costs too. Carpet of Flowers' 3 mana of one color therefore cannot pay the colored pip of three separate spells: the first spell eats all of it. Plan with this: cast a spell whose generic part can soak up the floating mana first, so the colored pip is paid from the remaining pool.

## Leave a spare mana for Daze; Brainstorm cost it here (Brady, game 2 of the interactive match)
With Carpet's 3 black plus lands I had exactly 9 mana for three Acererak casts, after spending 1 on Brainstorm earlier in the turn. The third cast was Dazed with no mana left to pay. Brady: skipping the Brainstorm would have left a spare mana to pay for Daze, and the line then loses only to Force of Will plus a blue card. Gaining 7 life from an unblocked Atraxa would also have mattered. When the plan is a chain of spells into open blue mana with Daze possible, count the Daze payment before spending mana on a cantrip.

## (Superseded, see the outcome entry below) Do not attack Atraxa into a board where the opponent's only threat is burn (Brady)
Attacking lets the opponent attack back. With Atraxa home as a deterrent and the opponent's hand empty, hang back and let the only real threat (Bolt) be the thing to play around.

## Outcome of the interactive game 2: the attack would have been right (Brady)
Brady first said to hold Atraxa back, then after the loss: "we should have attacked I think". Reasoning: attacking with Atraxa gains 7 life (lifelink) even if Murktide blocks and trades, and at 6 life the Pyroblast on Atraxa followed by Murktide + Monks was lethal. Before settling for "hang back", count what the opponent's topdecked removal does to the plan: if one removal spell on the blocker loses the game, the life gained by attacking matters.

## Pyroblast is a red removal spell: Veil of Summer does not stop it (Brady and I both missed it)
Veil gives hexproof from blue and black only. Pyroblast ("destroy target permanent if it's blue") is a red spell, so it kills Atraxa through Veil. The player brief should say: Pyroblast and Red Elemental Blast are red; only Force of Will, Daze, Counterspell-style cards are blue.

## Brady's summary of the main mistakes in interactive game 2
1. Shuffling away the known Carpet (fetching before the draw instead of after).
2. Casting Brainstorm on the Acererak turn. Brady notes it is a hard call because Brainstorm often wins the game there; without it a spare mana would have covered Daze.
3. Scrying Veil of Summer to the top (Veil does not stop Pyroblast, and it should have been put to Brady first).
4. Possibly the Ponder order (Ponder before the fetch/draw) as well, since Wasteland then removed the mana for Carpet that turn.

## Plan to cast Carpet of Flowers on turn 3 (Brady)
Always plan to cast Carpet on turn 3. It is really good into Wasteland (the mana comes from Islands the opponent controls, and Carpet is cheap to replay). If the opponent has no Wasteland, the mana from Carpet gets close to hardcasting Atraxa. This sets the turn-1-to-3 plan when keeping a hand and when ordering Ponder/fetch plays.
